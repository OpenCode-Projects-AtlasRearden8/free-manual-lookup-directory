# Archived Manual Libraries — third-party public archives

Third-party manuals sites range from wonderful to radioactive. Here are the ones worth your time, with honest reliability notes: **(reliability: X/10, last checked: 2026-11)**. Reliability here means: how much coverage of old/discontinued models, how usable without an account, and how sane the ads are.

## The good ones

| Library | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Internet Archive / Manual library collection** | The Internet Archive hosts a huge browsable library of owner's manuals and service guides scanned from physical collections: <https://archive.org/details/manuals> | Free, no account needed, plain scans with searchable OCR text. Coverage is broad across appliances, tools, and hardware from the '50s onward. Text-search inside scanned pages works better than you'd expect. *(9/10, 2026-11)* |
| **ManualsLib** | The largest free general manual library: <https://www.manualslib.com> | Millions of manuals; search by model number works well. Ad-supported — use a reader-mode browser toggle or a private window and don't click anything that says "Download Hotel Virus Free Now." *(7/10, 2026-11)* |
| **AppliancePartsPros manuals** | Parts site that attaches the manual and parts diagram to each part page: <https://www.appliancepartspros.com/parts-diagrams.html> | Genuinely useful for appliances: enter the model number, get the parts diagram plus the user manual for that exact model. You don't have to buy anything to view the diagrams. *(8/10, 2026-11)* |
| **Sears PartsDirect model lookup** | Model-number lookup that returns manual and exploded diagrams: <https://www.searspartsdirect.com> | Best for Kenmore, which no other site covers consistently since the brand passed around. Search by full model number from the tag. *(7/10, 2026-11)* |
| **Power tool & small engine enthusiast forums** | Brand-specific communities that maintain scanned manual archives | For pre-1980 power tools, mowers, and sewing machines, brand forums often beat everything. Not linked here — find the right community via a search template from `search-patterns.md`. *(varies)* |

## Use with care

| Library | Why the caution |
|---|---|
| **ManualsOnline** | Big library but account-walled for some downloads and ad-heavy: <https://www.manualsonline.com> *(5/10, 2026-11)* |
| **Upload-your-own manuals sites** | Several smaller sites ask users to *upload* manuals. We don't include them: that workflow chains you to other users' files. This directory only lists libraries that host verified scans. |

## Approach that always works for vintage or odd things

For pre-internet-era appliances (a 1962 Sunbeam mixer, a '70s air compressor with a mysterious cardinal paint job):

1. **Search the Internet Archive directly** — patterns in `search-patterns.md` (the vintage chapter) work well inside `archive.org` too, since the scans carry searchable OCR text.
2. **Try brand-enthusiast forums** — most long-lived tool and appliance brands (sewing machines, stand mixers, table saws) have maintained fan archives with full scans of decades of manuals.
3. **Museum and trade-association collections** — for certain categories, historical collections host scans free; the vintage chapter in `search-patterns.md` shows how to surface them.

## What we deliberately leave out

- Sites that require registration before showing you a PDF you could have downloaded directly. That friction is the opposite of this directory's purpose.
- Any site asking you to upload your own files, pay per download, or "sign up to continue" — those patterns mean your manual hunt becomes someone's lead funnel. Not here.
- Charge-per-download "manual stores." The manual is already free from the manufacturer or an archive 90% of the time.
